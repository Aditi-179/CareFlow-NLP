# CLINICAL REFERENCE: CARDIOVASCULAR HEALTH AND DISEASES

## 1. Overview of Cardiovascular Diseases (CVD)
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) represent a group of disorders involving the heart and blood vessels. They remain the leading cause of morbidity and premature mortality worldwide. Key conditions include Coronary Artery Disease (CAD), Myocardial Infarction (heart attack), Heart Failure, Cerebrovascular Accidents (stroke), Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD), and Cardiac Arrhythmias.

## 2. Common Risk Factors for Cardiovascular Disease
Cardiovascular risk factors are categorized into modifiable and non-modifiable factors:
- Modifiable Risk Factors:
  - Hypertension: Persistently elevated vascular wall shear stress leads to endothelial damage and atherosclerotic plaque deposition.
  - Dyslipidemia: Elevated Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C > 100 mg/dL), high triglycerides (> 150 mg/dL), and low High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C < 40 mg/dL in men, < 50 mg/dL in women).
  - Cigarette Smoking: Nicotine accelerates thrombogenesis, increases heart rate and blood pressure, causes coronary vasoconstriction, and induces chronic systemic vascular inflammation.
  - Diabetes and Insulin Resistance: Hyperglycemia induces oxidative stress, advanced glycation end products, and accelerates widespread atherogenesis.
  - Obesity and Central Adiposity: Excess visceral fat is metabolically active and secretes pro-inflammatory cytokines and adipokines that exacerbate vascular dysfunction.
  - Physical Inactivity and Sedentary Behavior.
  - Chronic Psychosocial Stress and Poor Sleep Quality (< 6 or > 9 hours nightly).
- Non-Modifiable Risk Factors:
  - Advanced Age: Increased vascular stiffness and accumulation of plaque over decades.
  - Male Sex or Post-Menopausal Female Status.
  - Family History of Premature CVD: First-degree male relative before age 55 or female relative before age 65.

## 3. Atherosclerosis and Myocardial Infarction
Atherosclerosis is the pathophysiologic foundation of most coronary heart disease. It begins with endothelial injury, followed by subendothelial accumulation of oxidized LDL particles, infiltration of monocytes differentiating into macrophages, and formation of foam cells leading to a fatty streak. Over years, a fibroatheromatous plaque develops. Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) or myocardial infarction occurs when an unstable atherosclerotic plaque ruptures or erodes, precipitating an acute occlusive thrombosis that cuts off oxygenated blood to the myocardium.

## 4. Warning Signs and Emergency Symptoms
Immediate emergency evaluation (calling emergency services) is mandatory if experiencing:
- Crushing, squeezing, heavy, or pressing substernal chest discomfort or pain.
- Pain radiating to the left shoulder, left arm, neck, jaw, back, or epigastrium.
- Sudden shortness of breath (dyspnea), with or without chest discomfort.
- Cold sweats (diaphoresis), lightheadedness, dizziness, presyncope, or unexplained nausea/vomiting.
- Women, older adults, and individuals with diabetes frequently present with atypical symptoms such as sudden profound fatigue, upper abdominal distress, or shortness of breath rather than classic substernal crushing pain.

## 5. Preventative Strategies and Lipid Management
- Regular Cardiovascular Screening: Fasting lipid panel, blood pressure screening every 1–2 years, fasting blood glucose or HbA1c, and calculating 10-year ASCVD risk scores.
- Mediterranean Diet: High consumption of extra virgin olive oil, nuts, seeds, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and fatty cold-water fish rich in Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA), with restricted ultra-processed meats and refined sugars.
- Lipid-Lowering Pharmacotherapy: Hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA) reductase inhibitors (statins such as Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin) provide plaque stabilization and significant LDL reduction. Non-statin agents include Ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors.
- Regular Physical Activity: 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise or 75–150 minutes of vigorous aerobic exercise per week.
